🌱 ABOUT US

GreenRoots Community Garden was founded in 2015 in Woodstock, Cape Town, with a clear and practical goal: transform underutilised urban land into productive, community-driven spaces that improve everyday life. At the time, the two plots that now form the foundation of GreenRoots were abandoned, neglected, and contributing nothing to the area. Today, those same spaces produce fresh vegetables, host educational programmes, and bring together a growing membership of over 300 people. This transformation didn’t happen because of funding alone or outside intervention—it happened because people showed up consistently and took ownership

Our Story

In the early days, GreenRoots was a small initiative driven by a handful of individuals who believed that access to fresh food shouldn’t depend on income level or location. The first phase involved clearing waste, restoring soil quality, and setting up basic infrastructure. Progress was slow, and there were challenges—limited resources, inconsistent participation, and environmental setbacks. But consistency built momentum. As more residents got involved, the gardens evolved into structured, community-managed spaces. Systems were introduced for planting cycles, harvesting, and fair distribution. Over time, partnerships with schools and local organisations helped expand both reach and impact. Today, GreenRoots stands as proof that small, consistent actions can produce long-term change.

Our Mission

Our mission is grounded in three key principles:

  • Building community connections
  • We create shared spaces where people from different backgrounds can work together toward a common goal.
  • Promoting sustainable living
  • We encourage practices that reduce waste, protect resources, and support long-term environmental health.
  • Ensuring fair access to fresh produce
  • Everyone deserves access to nutritious food, regardless of their financial situation.

How the Gardens Work

GreenRoots is not a service you consume—it’s a system you participate in.

Members contribute time and effort to the maintenance of the gardens. In return, they benefit from shared harvests, access to knowledge, and a stronger connection to their community.

Key activities include:

  • Soil preparation and composting
  • Planting seasonal crops
  • Water management and irrigation
  • Harvesting and distribution

This structure ensures that the gardens remain sustainable—not just environmentally, but socially.

Community Impact

The impact of GreenRoots goes beyond food production.

  • Families have more consistent access to fresh vegetables
  • Young people are exposed to practical environmental education
  • Community members build relationships that extend beyond the garden
  • Previously neglected spaces are now safe, productive environments

This is what real community development looks like—practical, visible, and ongoing Partnerships and Support

GreenRoots works with:

  • Local residents who form the backbone of the initiative
  • Schools that integrate gardening into their learning programmes
  • Corporate partners through CSR initiatives
  • Donors who support the long-term sustainability of the project

These partnerships are not symbolic—they contribute directly to operations, growth, and impact.
